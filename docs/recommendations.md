# Recommendations for the business

Based on two years of transactions (Dec 2009 to 9 Dec 2011, GBP). Figures marked *illustrative* are simple arithmetic on the data, not forecasts; each recommendation says how to test it.

## Summary

Revenue is flat to slightly up (+2.1%, interval -4.3% to +8.3%), highly seasonal (September to November = 37.5% of the year) and concentrated in a few trade-like accounts (top 10% of customers = 63% of net revenue). The practical agenda is to **protect the big accounts, win back the lapsed ones, plan around the autumn peak, and fix customer tracking** so the next analysis can say more.

## 1. Protect the 1,286 Champions

- **Evidence:** 22% of identified customers generate 69% of net revenue (about GBP 11.4M over two years, roughly GBP 5.7M a year).
- **Illustrative:** losing 5% of that value in a year would cost about GBP 0.28M.
- **Action:** named account owner, priority on stock for their usual lines, early notice of new ranges, a check-in when an account's orders slow.
- **How to test:** track order frequency of Champions month by month; alert when a customer's gap since last order exceeds twice their usual gap.

## 2. Run a win-back test on 820 'At risk' customers

- **Evidence:** 3+ orders each and silent for a long time; GBP 1.44M bought in total, so roughly GBP 0.7M a year at past rates.
- **Illustrative:** reactivating 10% of that annual value would be about GBP 70K.
- **Action:** personal contact with an offer matched to what they used to buy.
- **How to test (required before scaling):** contact a random half, leave the other half alone, compare orders over 90 days. Nothing in this data proves that contact lifts sales.

## 3. Plan stock, staff and cash around September to November

- **Evidence:** 37.5% of the last 12 months' revenue in three months; same shape in both years.
- **Action:** build stock and capacity before September; compare each quarter with the same quarter last year.
- **Planning figure:** the next 13 weeks (from 5 Dec 2011) are expected at about GBP 1.77M, with an 80% range of GBP 1.38M to GBP 2.42M. Past error of this method is 18.8%; update weekly and compare with actuals.

## 4. Capture a customer key on every order

- **Evidence:** 13.1% of sales revenue (GBP 2.58M) has no customer id.
- **Why it matters:** retention, lifetime value and win-back lists cover only 87% of revenue today.
- **Action:** require a customer or email key at checkout and on marketplace orders where possible.

## 5. Test ready-made sets for the strongest bundles

- **Evidence:** pairs such as the pink and blue polkadot cups (lift 36, 521 joint orders) are bought together far more than chance.
- **Caveat:** these are variants of one range bought by trade buyers, so this is a set-selling opportunity, not proof of cross-selling across unrelated items.
- **How to test:** offer the set at a multi-buy price to half of the buyers and compare basket value.

## 6. Review the highest-return products

- **Evidence:** returns are 2.4% of gross sales overall; among products above GBP 20K sales and 100 orders the highest rates are Fairy Cake Flannel Assorted Colour (26%), Feltcraft Doll Molly (18%) and Tea Time Party Bunting (17%).
- **Caveat:** returns are not linked to the original sale, so these rates are approximate.
- **Action:** inspect quality, description and packaging for these lines before reordering.

## What not to do with this analysis

- Do not use individual customer probabilities for decisions. On a later test period the model ranked customers no better than "recent activity" and over-predicted the total by 32%. Use segment totals only.
- Do not read the export-market growth rates (France +46%, Spain +32%) as a trend without checking whether one account explains them.

## What would sharpen it

Marketing spend and channel, unit costs (to move from revenue to profit), stock-outs, promotion calendar, and VAT-clear prices. With costs, the product and segment views could be re-ranked by profit instead of sales.
